CPCMS supports simultaneous public and private development lines with post hoc publication[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002], though the abstract does not detail the synchronization mechanism. The redactable signature approach by Brzuska et al. derives public repositories from private ones through redaction that removes sensitive subtrees while maintaining integrity of remaining data[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. Dewitt and Bennett's Git submodules approach physically separates private data on secure institutional drives from public code managed on collaborative platforms[P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017], using submodules as the linking mechanism for selective disclosure[P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017].

Pellegrini's distributed VCS encrypts content deltas and synchronizes between private clear repositories and public encrypted repositories[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. The public repository contains encrypted deltas, user public keys, and symmetric keys, with synchronization algorithms ensuring consistency[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Storage overhead includes the public repository size, key lists, and key maps, while computational overhead remains minimal except when revoking compromised keys[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

Bird et al. describe Git's inherent support for parallel repositories, where developers maintain private repositories that can be selectively made public through services like GitHub[C. Bird et al., 2009]. Git's distributed nature allows different content in private versus public repositories, with branches and merges managing different versions[C. Bird et al., 2009]. Git reduces storage and computational overhead compared to centralized systems through holistic compression and local metadata storage[C. Bird et al., 2009].

The remaining 16 studies did not address parallel repository architecture, focusing instead on redaction mechanisms, blockchain-based versioning, or other aspects of selective disclosure without specifying how private and public versions are maintained concurrently.

## Disclosure Policy Mechanisms

Only two studies provided explicit details about how per-commit policies control file disclosure. The vast majority of sources (19 of 21) did not describe disclosure policy mechanisms, manifests, or per-commit selection of publicly disclosed content.

gittuf implements disclosure policies through a metadata layer with a root of trust and cryptographically signed rule files[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. The system supports file-level, branch-level, and tag-level granularity of disclosure control[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Repository owners and developers can create and modify policies[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025], which are applied to commits through rule files and threshold mechanisms[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. The design supports dynamic policy changes through thresholds and delegations[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025], though policy conflict resolution is not explicitly mentioned[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025].

The studies on redactable signatures (Brzuska et al., Pöhls et al., Samelin et al., Haber et al., Derler et al.) focus on the cryptographic mechanisms for redaction rather than policy specification. Similarly, blockchain-based approaches (SEREDACT, EDGChain-E, CDEdit, Astrizi, Nandakumar et al.) emphasize modification control and verification but do not detail how policies determine which content should be publicly disclosed on a per-commit basis.

Merkle tree-based approaches dominate the verifiable linkage mechanisms. SEREDACT uses Merkle trees with efficient dynamic updating to verify authenticity and integrity of version checks[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023]. EDGChain-E embeds hash-based content identifiers in Merkle trees to enable transparent, auditable, and immutable tracking of data changes[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025], with SHA-256 hashing for IPFS content addressing and blockchain logs for verification by authorized parties[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. The system provides resistance to tampering through immutable logs and decentralized governance[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025].

gittuf's Reference State Log represents a notable architectural innovation: an authenticated, ordered, append-only log maintained collectively by all users[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Any party with repository access can verify authenticity, integrity, and completeness by checking RSL consistency using cryptographic signatures[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. The append-only nature and cryptographic authentication enable detection of attempts to drop, reorder, or tamper with log entries[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025].

Brzuska et al.'s redactable signatures employ an EU-CMA signature scheme that signs all edges in tree-structured data[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. Anyone with the public key can verify authenticity, integrity, and transparency by checking signatures on edges and the order of child nodes[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. The scheme is designed to be unforgeable based on standard cryptographic assumptions[C. Brzuska et al., 2010].

CDEdit uses chameleon hashes with ephemeral trapdoor (CHET) and ciphertext policy attribute-based encryption (CP-ABE) to ensure linkage[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. Any participant can verify authenticity, integrity, and completeness by checking the validity of hash pairs and signatures[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. The system resists tampering through digital signatures and CA-issued privilege tokens[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022].

Two systems limit verification to restricted parties. Astrizi's redactable blockchain allows only trusted authorities to verify authenticity and integrity[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022], while Nandakumar et al.'s sanitizable signatures restrict verification to the content creator[Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019]. Both rely on blockchain's immutability for tampering resistance[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022; Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019].

## Security Properties and Guarantees

The studies provide diverse security guarantees spanning integrity, privacy, authenticity, and accountability properties, with varying levels of formal verification.

**Integrity Properties**

Multiple studies ensure that specific elements cannot be tampered with. CPCMS uses cryptographic naming mechanisms for collision-free disconnected operation[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002]. Brzuska et al. ensure authenticity of remaining data after redaction, with unforgeability preventing unauthorized signature creation[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. Pöhls et al.'s RSS with signer commitment prohibits adding new elements after signature generation, protecting against malicious signers[H. Pöhls et al., 2012]. CDEdit provides indistinguishability and collision resistance properties[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. gittuf prevents unauthorized changes to branches, tags, and files/folders[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. UVHistory tracks file evolution to ensure history cannot be tampered with[David Reid & Audris Mockus, 2023]. Samelin et al. ensure structure and content cannot be independently tampered with[Kai Samelin et al., 2012].

**Privacy and Confidentiality Properties**

Fine-grained access control appears across multiple designs. CPCMS provides fine-grain access controls with simultaneous public and private development lines[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002]. SEREDACT uses restricted redaction policies to control data modification and disclosure[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023]. EDGChain-E employs end-to-end encryption to keep data hidden from unauthorized parties[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. Brzuska et al. ensure deleted data cannot be recovered and recipients cannot determine if parts have been redacted[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. Huang et al.'s TPCH integrates attribute-based encryption for policy-based access control[Ke Huang et al., 2026]. Lass et al.'s Mercurial extension maintains confidentiality through cryptography-enforced access control and end-to-end encryption[Michael Lass et al., 2016]. Pöhls et al. provide private mergeability and private redactable signatures[H. Pöhls et al., 2012]. Kundu addresses structural information leakage, which existing Merkle hash techniques fail to prevent[A. Kundu, 2010]. Derler et al. conceal original message length and redaction positions[David Derler et al., 2015]. Nandakumar et al. prevent reconstruction of redacted segments while retaining auxiliary data with the creator[Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019].

**Authenticity and Source Verification**

Cryptographic mechanisms provide source verification across designs. CPCMS, Lass et al., and Pellegrini all use cryptographic naming or digital signatures for authenticity[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002; Michael Lass et al., 2016; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. SEREDACT enables verifiable modification allowing users to verify source and update integrity[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023]. Brzuska et al.'s unforgeability ensures only authorized parties create valid signatures[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. CDEdit provides EUF-CMA security[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. gittuf uses cryptographic signatures to verify authenticity and ensure only authorized users make changes[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. UVHistory links code to its origin and version histories across projects[David Reid & Audris Mockus, 2023]. Haber et al. provide verifiable changes to signed data[Stuart Haber et al., 2008].

**Non-repudiation and Accountability**

Several systems provide accountability features. EDGChain-E signs and timestamps all transactions on the blockchain[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. Huang et al.'s TPCH provides publicly verifiable proofs of redaction and traceable user identities[Ke Huang et al., 2026]. Lass et al. and Pellegrini both use digital signatures for non-repudiation[Michael Lass et al., 2016; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Pöhls et al. provide transparency guarantees[H. Pöhls et al., 2012]. CDEdit uses CA-issued privilege tokens with final audit by CA[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. gittuf's RSL and policy states enable tracking changes and verifying validity of trusted keys or rules[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Pellegrini's signing of deltas allows tracing of changes with revocation process controlling access[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

**Formal Security Definitions**

Eight studies provide formal security definitions or proofs. Brzuska et al. provide proofs for unforgeability, privacy, and transparency[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. Pöhls et al. present a provably secure scheme[H. Pöhls et al., 2012]. CDEdit proves three theorems (5.1, 5.2, 5.3) covering indistinguishability, collision resistance, and EUF-CMA security[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. Samelin et al. describe their scheme as provably secure[Kai Samelin et al., 2012]. Kundu claims efficient and provably secure solutions[A. Kundu, 2010]. Derler et al. base their framework on EUF-CMA secure signature schemes[David Derler et al., 2015].

**Cryptographic Assumptions**

The studies rely on diverse cryptographic primitives. SEREDACT, EDGChain-E, Astrizi, and Nandakumar et al. all use Merkle trees[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023; Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025; T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022; Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019]. EDGChain-E and Pellegrini employ hybrid encryption combining symmetric and asymmetric schemes[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006], with Pellegrini specifically using AES and RSA[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Multiple systems use chameleon hash functions: TPCH, CDEdit, Astrizi, and Nandakumar et al.[Ke Huang et al., 2026; Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022; T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022; Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019]. CDEdit also integrates ciphertext policy attribute-based encryption (CP-ABE)[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. Derler et al. rely on indistinguishable cryptographic accumulators and non-interactive zero-knowledge proof systems[David Derler et al., 2015].

## Information Leakage Risks and Countermeasures

Information leakage analysis remains notably sparse in the literature. Only seven studies addressed leakage risks, with most focusing on specific threat vectors rather than comprehensive analysis.

**Structural Information Leakage**

Kundu identifies a critical vulnerability: existing Merkle hash techniques and redactable signature schemes leak structural information that can enable privacy and confidentiality breaches[A. Kundu, 2010]. The mitigation strategy involves efficient and provably secure solutions for trees, graphs, and forests, with support for encrypted data and leakage-free authentication of paths[A. Kundu, 2010]. Brzuska et al. address this by ensuring redacted parts are not recoverable and their removal is not detectable[C. Brzuska et al., 2010], using transparent and private structural signatures as mitigation[C. Brzuska et al., 2010].

**Retroactive Redaction and Collision Exposure**

Astrizi identifies that previous constructions reveal chameleon hash collisions when redacting blockchain content[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022]. The solution uses a subset of chameleon hash schemes where the trapdoor enables first-preimage computation, preventing collision exposure during redactions[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022]. Nandakumar et al. address retroactive redaction through sanitizable signature schemes that enable integrity verification without revealing redacted content[Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019]. The approach uses blockchain and robust hashing to securely manage sanitizable signatures[Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019].

**Merkle Tree-Based Protections**

SEREDACT addresses risks from file renames, moves, or structural changes through its Merkle tree-based verification mechanism[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023], implementing restricted redaction policies alongside the verification mechanism[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023]. EDGChain-E mitigates leakage through diffs by using encrypted Git patches[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025], with risks from structural changes addressed through recursive retrieval and patch application processes[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. The system implements client-side cryptography, immutable logs, and decentralized governance as mitigation strategies[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025].

**Version Control Specific Risks**

Bird et al. identify several Git-specific leakage vectors. Commits can be reordered, deleted, or edited, leading to potential information leakage[C. Bird et al., 2009]. The rebase feature allows rewriting history, though no specific retroactive redaction solutions are mentioned[C. Bird et al., 2009]. Potential risks exist from side-channel and inference attacks due to the ability to modify commit histories and track content changes[C. Bird et al., 2009]. The paper acknowledges difficulty in tracking merge sources and potential information leakage from Git's decentralized nature and history modification capabilities[C. Bird et al., 2009], but does not propose mitigation strategies.

**Encryption-Based Mitigations**

Pellegrini mitigates leakage through diffs by encrypting content deltas using symmetric ciphers[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Risks from file renames, moves, or structural changes are managed through a directed acyclic graph representing delta interdependencies[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. The mitigation strategies include encryption of deltas, access control mechanisms, and private-public repository synchronization[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

**Acknowledged Limitations**

Most studies do not acknowledge remaining vulnerabilities. SEREDACT, EDGChain-E, and Astrizi make no explicit mention of remaining vulnerabilities[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023; Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025; T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022]. Nandakumar et al., despite addressing retroactive redaction, do not mention remaining vulnerabilities[Karthik Nandakumar et al., 2019]. Pellegrini does not explicitly acknowledge vulnerabilities despite describing the system's limitations[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

Fourteen studies provided no information about information leakage risks, countermeasures, or related security analysis.

**Computational Performance**

CDEdit provides the most detailed computational benchmarks. The system demonstrates low performance overhead with average running times of 0.7s for KeyGen, 1.11s for Hash, and 1.2s for Adapt algorithms even with 100 attributes[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. Verification operations are particularly efficient, with Verify_m running between 0.0136ms and 0.042ms, Verify between 0.155ms and 0.198ms, and Audit between 0.34ms and 0.36ms[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. Performance scales linearly with attribute size and remains acceptable with up to 100 devices[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022].

Brzuska et al. provide theoretical complexity analysis showing O(n²) signature operations for general trees with large out-degrees[C. Brzuska et al., 2010], but achieve linear complexity for trees with bounded out-degree[C. Brzuska et al., 2010]. For binary trees specifically, the scheme requires exactly 3/2(\|V\|-1) + 1 signature operations[C. Brzuska et al., 2010].

Pellegrini's system achieves linear time complexity for most operations based on key size or change size[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. However, revoking compromised keys is affected by the number of users, and initial import depends on total content size[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

**Storage Efficiency**

gittuf demonstrates remarkably low storage overhead, ranging from only 0.70% to 3.62% for large repositories like Git and Kubernetes[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. EDGChain-E reduces storage overhead by transmitting only differences between versions[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. Bird et al. report that Git uses less space than CVS or SVN through holistic compression strategies[C. Bird et al., 2009]. Pellegrini notes additional storage requirements for the public repository size, key lists, and keymaps[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

**Scalability Analysis**

VCAligner achieves dramatic scalability improvements, reducing audit workload by approximately 158× from over 10.3 million commits to under 65,000 commits[Yan Li et al., 2026] in a dataset of 2,984 verifiable PyPI packages. gittuf proves viable even for large repositories with high activity volumes, requiring less than 0.59s to verify each push[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Bird et al. note Git is designed to handle large codebases and high contribution volumes[C. Bird et al., 2009].

**Verification Efficiency**

gittuf's verification times range from 0.0015s to 0.59s per push action in repositories like Git and Kubernetes[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. This represents minimal overhead for continuous verification. CDEdit's verification operations complete in under 1 millisecond for most operations[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022].

The remaining 15 studies provided no quantitative performance evaluation, benchmarks, or complexity analysis.

## Synthesis of Approaches and Limitations

The 21 studies reveal three distinct architectural paradigms for version control with selective disclosure, each with specific strengths and fundamental limitations.

**Redactable Signature Approaches (11 studies)**

Systems using redactable signatures (Brzuska et al., Pöhls et al., Samelin et al., Haber et al., Derler et al., SEREDACT, TPCH, CDEdit, Astrizi, Kundu, Nandakumar et al.) focus on post-publication modification rather than parallel repository maintenance. These schemes enable removing or modifying signed content while preserving signature validity, but they fundamentally operate on a single repository model where content is retrospectively redacted.

The key limitation is computational complexity. Brzuska et al. acknowledge O(n²) signature operations for general trees[C. Brzuska et al., 2010], which becomes prohibitive at scale. Their scheme achieves linear complexity only for trees with bounded out-degree[C. Brzuska et al., 2010], restricting applicability. Kundu addresses structural information leakage that plagues Merkle hash approaches[A. Kundu, 2010], but provides no parallel repository architecture.

Security varies substantially. Eight schemes provide formal proofs (Brzuska, Pöhls, CDEdit, Samelin, Kundu, Derler)[C. Brzuska et al., 2010; H. Pöhls et al., 2012; Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022; Kai Samelin et al., 2012; A. Kundu, 2010; David Derler et al., 2015], while others lack formal verification. Astrizi's innovation—preventing collision exposure during redaction[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022]—solves a critical vulnerability in chameleon hash schemes, but verification remains restricted to trusted authorities[T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022].

Practical deployment faces obstacles. CDEdit is the only redactable blockchain with comprehensive performance benchmarks[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022], demonstrating feasibility for attribute sizes up to 100[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022]. However, CDEdit's reliance on trusted Certificate Authorities for privilege tokens[Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022] introduces centralization that may not hold in adversarial environments. TPCH acknowledges suitability only for small-scale deployment[Ke Huang et al., 2026].

**Cryptographic Access Control in Distributed VCS (4 studies)**

The Mercurial extension (Lass et al.), Pellegrini's Apso, Dewitt's Git submodules, and CPCMS represent a distinct paradigm: encrypting content within existing distributed version control systems. This approach maintains genuinely parallel repositories—private content exists in plaintext for authorized users, while unauthorized parties receive encrypted versions.

Pellegrini's system provides the most complete architecture description. The public repository stores encrypted deltas, user public keys, and symmetric keys[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006], with a directed acyclic graph managing delta interdependencies[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Most operations achieve linear time complexity[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006], but key revocation scales poorly with user count[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Storage overhead includes the public repository, key lists, and keymaps[Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006].

The Mercurial extension integrates end-to-end encryption, digital signatures, convergent encryption, and content-defined chunking[Michael Lass et al., 2016] while maintaining compatibility with existing deployments[Michael Lass et al., 2016]. However, distributed replication introduces security complications[Michael Lass et al., 2016], and neither this system nor Pellegrini's provides mechanisms for per-commit disclosure policies.

Dewitt's Git submodules offer a lightweight alternative: private data resides on secure institutional drives while public code lives on collaborative platforms[P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017], linked via submodules for selective disclosure[P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017]. This maintains physical separation but provides no cryptographic guarantees and no performance data[P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017].

CPCMS claims simultaneous public and private development lines with post hoc publication[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002; J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002], but the abstract provides insufficient detail on implementation, verification mechanisms, or security properties.

**Blockchain-Based Versioning (5 studies)**

EDGChain-E, DCGit, and aspects of SEREDACT, CDEdit, and Astrizi leverage blockchain for immutability and decentralized governance. EDGChain-E provides the most complete implementation, integrating Git versioning with blockchain and IPFS[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. Initial commits capture full encrypted datasets, while subsequent updates use encrypted Git patches[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025], reducing storage overhead by transmitting only differences[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025].

EDGChain-E addresses parallel repository needs through client-side encryption: encrypted data goes to IPFS/blockchain, while authorized parties decrypt locally. Merkle trees with SHA-256 content identifiers provide verifiable linkage[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025], with immutable logs and decentralized governance preventing tampering[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025]. However, the paper lacks performance benchmarks[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025] and does not detail per-commit disclosure policies[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025].

DCGit similarly uses Ethereum and IPFS[Pratham Gupta et al., 2023] but acknowledges severe limitations: blockchain storage creates scalability issues[Pratham Gupta et al., 2023], and blockchain's open nature exacerbates data privacy concerns[Pratham Gupta et al., 2023]. The abstract provides no technical details on architecture, performance, or security properties.

**Orthogonal Approaches**

Four studies address related but distinct problems. Bird et al. analyze Git's existing features, identifying leakage through commit reordering, deletion, and editing[C. Bird et al., 2009], with difficulty tracking merge sources[C. Bird et al., 2009]. Git's distributed nature inherently supports parallel repositories[C. Bird et al., 2009], but provides no selective disclosure mechanism.

VCAligner solves supply chain provenance by mapping released artifacts to originating commits[Yan Li et al., 2026], reducing audit workload by ~158×[Yan Li et al., 2026], but does not address parallel repositories or selective disclosure.

gittuf decentralizes Git security through a Reference State Log that all users maintain collectively[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Any party with repository access can verify authenticity, integrity, and completeness[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Storage overhead remains minimal (0.70%-3.62%)[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025] with verification times under 0.59s per push[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. However, gittuf requires universal adoption[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025] and transitioning from SHA-1[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. The system implements file-level, branch-level, and tag-level disclosure policies[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025], but does not explicitly support parallel private/public repositories.

UVHistory links code to version histories across projects[David Reid & Audris Mockus, 2023], enabling identification of bugs, vulnerabilities, and license violations[David Reid & Audris Mockus, 2023], but addresses neither parallel repositories nor selective disclosure.

**Critical Gaps**

The literature exhibits fundamental gaps. Only 5 of 21 studies describe parallel repository architectures[J. Shapiro & J. Vanderburgh, 2002; C. Brzuska et al., 2010; P. Dewitt & T. Bennett, 2017; C. Bird et al., 2009; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Only 2 specify disclosure policy mechanisms[Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025]. Information leakage analysis appears in just 7 studies[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023; Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025; C. Brzuska et al., 2010; C. Bird et al., 2009; T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006; A. Kundu, 2010], with only Kundu and Brzuska et al. addressing structural information leakage comprehensively[C. Brzuska et al., 2010; A. Kundu, 2010].

Performance evaluation is sparse: only 6 studies provide quantitative metrics[Alper Alimoglu et al., 2025; Yan Li et al., 2026; C. Brzuska et al., 2010; Xiaofeng Chen & Yinghu Gao, 2022; Aditya Sirish A Yelgundhalli et al., 2025; C. Bird et al., 2009; Jerônimo Pellegrini, 2006]. Most theoretical constructions (10 studies)[Zhuo Xu et al., 2023; C. Brzuska et al., 2010; Ke Huang et al., 2026; H. Pöhls et al., 2012; T. Astrizi & Ricardo Felipe Custódio, 2022; David Derler et al., 2015] lack implementation or benchmarks.

The integration between redactable signatures and version control remains unsolved. Redactable signature schemes operate on tree-structured or graph-structured data but do not map cleanly to Git's content-addressable storage model. No study demonstrates how to maintain commit graph integrity while selectively redacting file contents across branches.
