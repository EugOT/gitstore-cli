Scale varied considerably, from small controlled experiments to massive analyses covering millions of repositories.

## Quantitative Measures for Repository Divergence and Concordance

### Entropy-Based Measures

**Fork Entropy.** Two studies by Zhiwen Zheng et al. introduced fork entropy as a measure of diversity in fork populations[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a]. The metric is based on Rao's quadratic entropy with a distance function defined on forks' modifications to project files[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a]. Fork entropy captures modifications to project files across different forks[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a] at file-level granularity[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a]. The computational approach involves constructing a file modification matrix from commit history, then applying Rao's quadratic entropy formula with a Laplacian kernel and adjustable hyperparameter γ to control sensitivity[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a]. Both studies validated the metric on fifty GitHub projects[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a], finding significant correlations with external productivity, pull-request acceptance rates, and code quality[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a]. The fork entropy metric demonstrated properties including symmetry, continuity, and monotonicity[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a], and showed that fork diversity enriches understanding beyond simple fork counts[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a].

**Convergence Entropy.** Yang Shen et al. proposed convergence entropy to quantify integration effectiveness in fork-based development[Yang Shen et al., 2026]. This metric measures similarity between distributions of original and merged commits across forks, adjusted for integration ratio[Yang Shen et al., 2026]. It captures not only volume of contributions but also their diversity and coordination[Yang Shen et al., 2026], operating at commit-level granularity[Yang Shen et al., 2026]. The study found significant correlations with three dimensions of OSS project productivity[Yang Shen et al., 2026].

**Information-Theoretic Entropy.** Adriano Torres et al. introduced textual and structural entropy measures for tracking code evolution[Adriano Torres et al., 2025; Adriano Torres et al., 2023]. Textual entropy measures information content based on token frequencies, while structural entropy uses abstract syntax tree nodes[Adriano Torres et al., 2025]. Both operate at commit and file levels[Adriano Torres et al., 2025; Adriano Torres et al., 2023]. The computational approach uses Shannon entropy calculation[Adriano Torres et al., 2025], with preprocessing via PyDriller for commit traversal, JavaParser for AST histograms, and Spiral library for tokenization[Adriano Torres et al., 2025]. In a study of 95 Java projects[Adriano Torres et al., 2025], the measures showed weak to moderate correlations with traditional complexity metrics[Adriano Torres et al., 2023], but achieved over 60% precision in anomaly detection[Adriano Torres et al., 2025] with true positive ratios of 72.50% and 81.13% for different settings[Adriano Torres et al., 2025]. The two entropy types exhibited high correlation (0.98 Spearman correlation between entropy deltas and expected outcomes)[Adriano Torres et al., 2023] but both showed weak correlations with traditional metrics like cyclomatic complexity[Adriano Torres et al., 2023].

### Distance and Divergence Measures

**Edit Distance Metrics.** Several studies employed edit distance approaches at different granularities. Canfora et al. applied Levenshtein edit distance at line-level for CVS repositories[G. Canfora et al., 2007; G. Canfora et al., 2007], combining it with information retrieval techniques[G. Canfora et al., 2007] and achieving high precision and recall on the ArgoUML case study[G. Canfora et al., 2007]. Beat Fluri et al. developed change distilling, a tree differencing algorithm for fine-grained source code change extraction[Beat Fluri et al., 2007], which approximated the minimum edit script 45% better than the original Chawathe et al. approach, with mean absolute percentage error of 34% versus 79%[Beat Fluri et al., 2007; Beat Fluri et al., 2007].

**Branch Drift Metrics.** Karl Kegel et al. introduced two related drift metrics for branch-based development. The branch-drift metric summarizes merge conflict potential in a numeric value[Karl Kegel et al., 2025], configured to fit project requirements[Karl Kegel et al., 2025] and validated on 21 large-scale repositories over three months[Karl Kegel et al., 2025; Karl Kegel et al., 2025]. The related Drift metric for model variants quantifies inconsistency using mean absolute median deviation of model embeddings in Euclidean space[Karl Kegel et al., 2024; Karl Kegel et al., 2024], operating at line-level on branches[Karl Kegel et al., 2024]. Validation on synthetic modeling projects showed correctness but revealed quadratic runtime growth with number of variants[Karl Kegel et al., 2024]. The Driftool implementation provides interactive visualization with drift plots and scatterplots[Karl Kegel et al., 2024].

**Phylogenetic Distance Matrices.** Mori and Hashimoto applied phylogenetic techniques from computational biology to software evolution[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023]. Their approach uses tree differencing on abstract syntax trees to calculate distance matrices[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023], which are then processed by distance-based phylogenetic algorithms (Neighbor-Joining, Neighbor-Net) to infer evolution networks[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023; A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023]. The method uses normalized edit distance as a ratio of edit distance to mapped nodes[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023]. Experiments on Emacs and 3D printer firmware successfully reproduced software evolution and identified code import/merging across lineages[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023]. Visualizations included phylograms, reticulograms, and split networks[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023].

### Similarity and Concordance Measures

**Repository Embeddings.** Rokon et al. developed Repo2Vec, which represents repositories as distributed vectors by combining metadata, structure, and source code features[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021]. The approach uses meta2vec, struct2vec, and source2vec with cosine similarity[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021], achieving 93% precision for general similarity and 98% precision for malware versus benign classification[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021; Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021]. This significantly outperformed CrossSim's 78% precision[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021], with nearly twice as many strongly similar repositories identified[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021].

**Semantic Similarity.** Ping Ma et al. proposed cosine and doc2vec methods to quantify structural and semantic similarity between pull requests that change the same code[Ping Ma et al., 2017; Ping Ma et al., 2017]. Applied to four Java projects[Ping Ma et al., 2017], the study found high similarity between competing pull requests[Ping Ma et al., 2017], though diversity existed across projects[Ping Ma et al., 2017]. Naohiro Kawamitsu et al. used longest common subsequence (LCS) similarity for identifying code reuse relationships between repositories[Naohiro Kawamitsu et al., 2014; Naohiro Kawamitsu et al., 2014], analyzing eight project pairs and identifying 1,394 file reuse instances, with 75.3% recorded and 20.1% unrecorded but recovered by their approach[Naohiro Kawamitsu et al., 2014; Naohiro Kawamitsu et al., 2014].

**Version-Aware Novelty.** Rinas and Wickramasinghe introduced a version-aware novelty metric integrating structural dissimilarity with pattern rarity[A.M. Rinas & B. Wickramasinghe, 2026]. The metric produces bounded values reflecting uniqueness and structural innovation[A.M. Rinas & B. Wickramasinghe, 2026], defining three measures: global novelty (distinctiveness relative to repository history), initial drift (cumulative deviation from first implementation), and local change (immediate modifications between consecutive versions)[A.M. Rinas & B. Wickramasinghe, 2026]. The methodology is language-agnostic, computationally efficient, and compatible with standard Git workflows[A.M. Rinas & B. Wickramasinghe, 2026].

### Clone Detection and Tracking Measures

**Clone Detection Accuracy Metrics.** Multiple studies used precision and recall to evaluate clone detection performance. ForkSim, a tool for generating synthetic software forks with known similarities[Jeffrey Svajlenko et al., 2013], allows measurement of recall and precision by comparing tool output to known properties[Jeffrey Svajlenko et al., 2013; Jeffrey Svajlenko et al., 2013]. LVMapper, designed for large-variance clone detection, achieved 88.5% precision on BigCloneBench[Ming Wu et al., 2019] with nearly 100% recall for Type-1, Type-2, and Very Strongly Type-3 clones[Ming Wu et al., 2019], detecting more than five times as many large-variance clones as CCAligner[Ming Wu et al., 2019]. The tool scales from 1M to 250M lines of code[Ming Wu et al., 2019; Ming Wu et al., 2019].

**Clone Genealogy Metrics.** Ripon Saha et al. developed metrics for tracking clone evolution across versions. Their approach uses longest common subsequence count (LCSC) similarity metrics for function mapping[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011; Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011], achieving precision and recall of 1.0 in identity tests and at least 98% in manual validation[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011]. The gCad prototype significantly outperformed iClones in speed, handling large systems like Linux Kernel where iClones faced memory issues[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011]. The tool was adaptable to different clone detection tools and scalable[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011], with execution times ranging from less than a second to a couple of minutes[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011].

**Cross-Language Clone Detection.** Ankali and Parthiban employed Levenshtein distance for detecting functionally equivalent code clones across C, C++, and Java[Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021]. Their keyword-based filtering approach achieved 100% precision for exact and renamed clones, 98% for near-miss clones, and 95% for semantic clones[Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021], representing the best accuracy for cross-language clone detection at the time[Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021].

### N-Way and Multi-Variant Comparison

**Set-Based N-Way Diff.** Duszynski et al. developed an N-way diff approach representing comparison results as models of N intersecting sets[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020]. The method aggregates sets along the system decomposition hierarchy (files, folders, whole systems)[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020], with visualizations scaling to more than ten component variants and MLOC-sized components[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020]. In a controlled experiment comparing five software forks, the set-based approach reduced task completion time by 58% and incorrect answers by 92% compared to pairwise comparison[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020; Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020].

**Deforking Maps.** Audris Mockus created a deforking map for the World of Code using hub-node star encoding and parallel Louvain clustering on 51.79M shared-commit groups[Audris Mockus, 2026; Audris Mockus, 2026]. The map collapses repositories into deforked projects based on shared commit history[Audris Mockus, 2026; Audris Mockus, 2026], with capped variants to bound mega-cluster size[Audris Mockus, 2026]. Validation against GitHub's fork graph showed 99.01% edge agreement[Audris Mockus, 2026], with disagreements primarily representing multi-forge families (5.41%) and forks not rooted on GitHub (1.51%)[Audris Mockus, 2026]. Spinellis et al. created a similar dataset of 10.6M GitHub project copies using geometric mean ranking of six quality attributes[D. Spinellis et al., 2020; D. Spinellis et al., 2020], identifying 30,000 duplicate projects in a reference dataset[D. Spinellis et al., 2020].

**Hierarchical Set Models.** Duszynski's dissertation proposed modeling variants as hierarchical intersecting sets of uniquely identifiable elements[Slawomir Duszynski, 2015], expressing similarity using set algebra[Slawomir Duszynski, 2015]. This approach provides similarity information at any abstraction level from single code lines to whole products[Slawomir Duszynski, 2015], supporting simultaneous analysis of multiple variants[Slawomir Duszynski, 2015].

### Patch and Change Analysis Measures

**Patch Propagation Metrics.** PaReco uses clone detection with n-gram analysis (n=4) to identify redundant and missed patches across variants[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022; Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. Applied to 364 variant pairs with 8,323 patches[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022], the tool achieved 91% precision, 80% recall, 88% accuracy, and 85% F1-score[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. The study found missed patches were introduced in source variants an average of 52 weeks earlier than their potential application in target variants[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. RePatch extends this work by handling refactoring-aware patch integration across structurally divergent Java forks[Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025]. While Git cherry-pick failed in 64.4% of cases due to structural misalignments, RePatch successfully integrated 52.8% of previously failing patches[Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025; Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025].

**Redundancy Detection.** Ren et al. developed machine learning approaches using AdaBoost to identify redundant code changes in forks[Luyao Ren et al., 2019; Luyao Ren et al., 2019]. Features included Term Frequency Inverse Document Frequency, Latent Semantic Indexing, Vector Space Model, Jaccard similarity, code change location similarity, and issue tracker references[Luyao Ren et al., 2019]. The approach achieved 57-83% precision from the maintainer perspective[Luyao Ren et al., 2019] and saved developers 1.9-3.0 commits on average[Luyao Ren et al., 2019], outperforming state-of-the-art by 16-21% in recall[Luyao Ren et al., 2019].

**Co-Change Detection.** Antoniol et al. used dynamic time warping to detect groups of co-changing files in CVS repositories[G. Antoniol et al., 2005; G. Antoniol et al., 2005]. Lozano et al. introduced macro co-changes, grouping bursts of changes closer to each other than to other changes regardless of author or commit message[A. Lozano et al., 2014]. Their analysis found that most cloned files macro co-change only with files sharing clones[A. Lozano et al., 2014], contradicting previous evidence of 50% consistency rates[A. Lozano et al., 2014].

### Semantic and Behavioral Measures

**Semantic Graph Differencing.** The Dex tool applies graph differencing to abstract semantic graphs representing each version[Shruti Raghavan et al., 2004; Shruti Raghavan et al., 2004]. Applied to Apache and GCC bug fixes[Shruti Raghavan et al., 2004], it produces summary statistics characterizing changes and identifies higher-level program changes[Shruti Raghavan et al., 2004]. Hashimoto and Mori developed code homology analysis using fine-grained tree differencing on abstract syntax trees[M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010; M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010]. Their Diff/TS tool achieved a similarity score of 2m/t (where m is matches and t is total change types)[M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010], successfully tracking clone regions across multiple versions[M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010] and processing entire Linux device driver subsystems in 3-5 days[M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010].

**Feature Identification.** INFOX uses community detection and TF-IDF scoring to automatically identify non-merged features in forks[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018; Shurui Zhou et al., 2018]. The clustering achieved 90% accuracy on known features[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018], improving over ClusterChanges by 54-92%[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018]. Human-subject evaluation showed participants agreed with clustering results and found the tool useful[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018].

**Opinion Dynamics Modeling.** He et al. integrated code embeddings with opinion dynamics theory using the Expressed-Private Opinion model[Yulong He et al., 2026]. They encoded code snippets using state-of-the-art models (e.g., intfloat/e5-base-v2), applied PCA for dimensionality reduction, and derived trust matrices to track opinion trajectories[Yulong He et al., 2026]. Analysis of three GitHub repositories revealed interpretable behavioral trends[Yulong He et al., 2026], with PCA demonstrating superior preservation of local structures compared to UMAP, LLE, and MDS[Yulong He et al., 2026].

### Complexity and Evolution Proxies

**Indentation as Complexity Proxy.** Hindle et al. proposed using statistical moments of indentation (standard deviation and summation) as a lightweight, language-independent proxy for code complexity[Abram Hindle et al., 2008]. Applied to 278 SourceForge projects[Abram Hindle et al., 2008], the approach showed linear and rank correlation with traditional complexity metrics[Abram Hindle et al., 2008], offering a cheap alternative for ranking revisions by complexity[Abram Hindle et al., 2008].

**Commit Similarity.** Ochodek et al. compared SimSAX (DNA-motifs-inspired) with k-Means and Hierarchical clustering for calculating similarity between projects based on Git commit series[Mirosław Ochodek et al., 2020; Mirosław Ochodek et al., 2020]. SimSAX provided more accurate results than clustering algorithms, which were sensitive to parameters and often found incorrect similarities[Mirosław Ochodek et al., 2020; Mirosław Ochodek et al., 2020].

**Product Evolution Trees.** Kanda et al. constructed Product Evolution Trees as minimum spanning trees using file similarity based on longest common subsequence[Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013; Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013]. Applied to six open-source datasets[Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013], 53-92% of edges in extracted trees were consistent with actual evolution history[Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013]. The approach requires normalizing source files into token sequences and calculating similarity with a threshold of 0.9 for similar file pairs[Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013].

## Visualization Techniques

Visualization approaches varied considerably across studies, ranging from traditional plots to specialized interactive tools.

**Interactive Clone Evolution Visualization.** ClonEvol combined Subversion versioning information with Doxygen static analysis and Simian clone detection[Avdo Hanjalic, 2013], visualizing results using mirrored radial trees with hierarchically bundled edges showing clone relations[Avdo Hanjalic, 2013]. The tool supported scrolling through time and filtering nodes by type[Avdo Hanjalic, 2013], processing 5,160 versions of FileZilla in approximately 11:45 hours[Avdo Hanjalic, 2013]. The main bottleneck was scope extraction for C/C++ projects[Avdo Hanjalic, 2013].

**Drift Visualization.** The Driftool provides drift plots showing Euclidean point clouds and scatterplots with interactive reports and visual feedback[Karl Kegel et al., 2024]. Branch-drift metrics were visualized through quantitative analysis of trends and patterns over time for 21 repositories[Karl Kegel et al., 2025].

**Metric Evolution Maps.** Silva et al. proposed Metric Evolution Maps creating dynamic maps showing entity similarity within revisions and changes across revisions[R. O. D. Silva et al., 2016]. Visual cues indicated key metrics and values driving pattern formation[R. O. D. Silva et al., 2016].

**Live Scatter Plots.** Cordy used live scatter plots with pop-up information for rapidly exposing and analyzing similarities between systems[J. Cordy, 2011].

**Phylogenetic Visualizations.** Mori and Hashimoto employed phylograms, reticulograms, and split networks to represent evolutionary relationships[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023]. Continua visualization represented lifetimes of AST nodes showing introduction and removal over time[M. Hashimoto & A. Mori, 2010]. SplitsTree and T-REX web server were used for generating these visualizations[A. Mori & M. Hashimoto, 2023].

**Statistical and Temporal Visualizations.** Rokon et al. used bar charts, hierarchical clustering diagrams, and elbow plots[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021]. Ramkisoen et al. employed boxplots, violin plots, grouped bar plots, and line plots to show technical lag over time[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. Torres et al. integrated entropy evolution visualizations directly into IDEs and version control systems[Adriano Torres et al., 2025], showing entropy changes through graphs over time[Adriano Torres et al., 2025].

**Feature Mapping.** Zhou et al. created web pages summarizing features with keywords and color mapping to source code, with navigation buttons to jump between code fragments[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018].

Several studies noted no specific visualization techniques in their abstracts[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a; Yang Shen et al., 2026; G. Canfora et al., 2007; Beat Fluri et al., 2007; Ping Ma et al., 2017; Tibor Bakota, 2011; Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025; A.M. Rinas & B. Wickramasinghe, 2026; Shruti Raghavan et al., 2004; G. Antoniol et al., 2005; Abram Hindle et al., 2008; Mirosław Ochodek et al., 2020; Alex C. Fish et al., 2018; Naohiro Kawamitsu et al., 2014; Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021; Thierry Lavoie & E. Merlo, 2013; A. Lozano et al., 2014].

**Availability.** Open-source implementations were released for several tools including Repo2Vec[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021], PaReco[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022], entropy-based measures[Adriano Torres et al., 2025; Adriano Torres et al., 2023], Driftool[Karl Kegel et al., 2024], phylogenetic analysis code[A. ## Performance and Validation

**Precision and Recall.** Precision values ranged from 57-98% across different measure types and domains. Repo2Vec achieved 93% precision for general similarity[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021], while cross-language clone detection reached 95-100% precision depending on clone type[Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021]. PaReco achieved 91% precision with 80% recall[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. Redundancy detection showed 57-83% precision from maintainer perspective[Luyao Ren et al., 2019]. LVMapper achieved 88.5% precision on BigCloneBench[Ming Wu et al., 2019]. Clone genealogy extraction achieved precision and recall of 1.0 in identity tests[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011]. INFOX achieved 90% accuracy on known features[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018].

**Comparative Performance.** Several studies demonstrated performance improvements over baselines. Repo2Vec outperformed CrossSim by 15 percentage points in precision (93% vs 78%)[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021; Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021], with nearly twice as many strongly similar repositories identified[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021]. Change distilling approximated minimum edit script 45% better than the Chawathe et al. baseline, with 34% versus 79% mean absolute percentage error[Beat Fluri et al., 2007; Beat Fluri et al., 2007]. Set-based N-way comparison reduced task time by 58% and incorrect answers by 92% compared to pairwise comparison[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020; Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020]. INFOX improved accuracy over ClusterChanges by 54-92%[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018]. Redundancy detection outperformed state-of-the-art by 16-21% in recall[Luyao Ren et al., 2019; Luyao Ren et al., 2019].

**Accuracy Validation.** Deforking maps showed 99.01% edge agreement with GitHub's fork graph[Audris Mockus, 2026]. Entropy-based anomaly detection achieved true positive ratios of 72.50% and 81.13% for different settings[Adriano Torres et al., 2025], with Spearman correlation of 0.98 between entropy deltas and expected outcomes[Adriano Torres et al., 2023]. Product Evolution Trees showed 53-92% edge consistency with actual evolution history across datasets[Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013].

**Scalability.** Tools demonstrated varying scalability characteristics. LVMapper scaled from 1M to 250M lines of code[Ming Wu et al., 2019; Ming Wu et al., 2019], being fastest for 1M-30M LOC datasets[Ming Wu et al., 2019]. The World of Code deforking map processed 51.79M shared-commit groups[Audris Mockus, 2026]. Spinellis et al. analyzed 10.6M GitHub projects[D. Spinellis et al., 2020]. Set-based visualization scaled to more than ten variants and MLOC-sized components[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020]. gCad handled large systems like Linux Kernel where competing tools faced memory issues[Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011]. Torres et al. analyzed 95 open-source projects for entropy evolution[Adriano Torres et al., 2025].

**Validation Methodologies.** Studies employed diverse validation approaches including controlled experiments[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020], synthetic datasets with known properties[Jeffrey Svajlenko et al., 2013], manual validation[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022; Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011], comparison against ground truth[Audris Mockus, 2026; Tetsuya Kanda et al., 2013], and human-subject evaluations[Shurui Zhou et al., 2018]. Benchmarks included BigCloneBench for clone detection[Ming Wu et al., 2019] and manually classified changes for change extraction[Beat Fluri et al., 2007].

## Synthesis

The 40 studies reveal substantial heterogeneity in both the types of measures proposed and their validation contexts, requiring careful synthesis to reconcile seemingly disparate findings.

**Measure Type by Granularity and Purpose.** Different measures excel in different contexts based on three key dimensions: (1) granularity level (line, file, or repository), (2) temporal scope (snapshot, pairwise, or continuous evolution), and (3) semantic depth (syntactic, structural, or behavioral).

Entropy-based measures (fork entropy[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a], convergence entropy[Yang Shen et al., 2026], information-theoretic entropy[Adriano Torres et al., 2025; Adriano Torres et al., 2023]) excel at capturing aggregate diversity and change intensity across variant populations. Fork entropy's significant correlations with productivity and quality metrics[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a] demonstrate effectiveness for repository-level analysis, while structural and textual entropy's weak correlations with traditional complexity metrics[Adriano Torres et al., 2023] suggest they capture different complexity dimensions - measuring information content rather than control flow. These measures are particularly effective for continuous evolution monitoring and anomaly detection[Adriano Torres et al., 2025], where their high correlation (0.98) between textual and structural variants[Adriano Torres et al., 2023] indicates robust signal regardless of representation choice.

Distance metrics show bifurcation by granularity. Line-level edit distances[G. Canfora et al., 2007] and tree-based change distilling[Beat Fluri et al., 2007] provide fine-grained change extraction, with change distilling achieving 45% better approximation of minimum edit script than baselines[Beat Fluri et al., 2007]. However, this precision comes at computational cost, requiring specialized parsing infrastructure. Conversely, repository-level distance metrics like Repo2Vec[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021] sacrifice fine-grained precision for broad applicability, achieving 93% precision[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021] across diverse metadata, structural, and code features without requiring complete syntactic analysis. The choice between these approaches depends on whether the use case requires understanding specific code changes (use tree differencing) or identifying similar repositories at scale (use embedding-based approaches).

Clone detection measures demonstrate the clearest case of context-dependent performance. Studies achieving 95-100% precision[Sanjay B. Ankali & L. Parthiban, 2021; Ripon K. Saha et al., 2011] focused on exact and near-miss clones with controlled structural changes, while those detecting large-variance clones with scattered modifications achieved lower precision (88.5%)[Ming Wu et al., 2019] despite detecting five times more clones than alternatives[Ming Wu et al., 2019]. This reflects a fundamental precision-recall tradeoff: strict similarity thresholds miss divergent variants, while relaxed thresholds produce false positives. LVMapper's approach of dynamic thresholds based on block size[Ming Wu et al., 2019] represents one solution, adapting sensitivity to context.

**Pairwise Versus N-Way Comparison Trade-offs.** The controlled experiment showing 58% time reduction and 92% fewer errors for set-based N-way comparison versus pairwise[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020; Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020] might suggest N-way approaches universally dominate. However, this advantage applies specifically to similarity assessment across many variants. For detailed change understanding between two specific versions, pairwise tree differencing[Beat Fluri et al., 2007] provides richer semantic information about edit sequences and change types. The measures are thus complementary: use N-way for variant landscape overview and pairwise for focused change analysis.

**Deforking and Repository Deduplication Accuracy.** The World of Code deforking map's 99.01% edge agreement with GitHub[Audris Mockus, 2026] appears to validate shared-commit-based clustering. However, the 5.41% multi-forge families and 1.51% non-GitHub-rooted forks that the method uniquely identifies[Audris Mockus, 2026] represent genuine additional coverage rather than disagreement. The cap250/cap500 variants bounding mega-cluster size[Audris Mockus, 2026] explicitly trade precision for robustness, preventing boilerplate hubs from creating spurious connections. This demonstrates that "accuracy" requires context: platform-specific graph reconstruction differs from cross-platform shared-history analysis, and both are valid depending on research question. Studies requiring GitHub-centric social relationships should use platform APIs; those studying code reuse across forges should use shared-commit approaches.

**Entropy Correlation Patterns and Interpretation.** Information-theoretic entropy measures consistently show weak correlations with traditional complexity metrics[Adriano Torres et al., 2025; Adriano Torres et al., 2023] but strong mutual correlation (0.98)[Adriano Torres et al., 2023]. This pattern indicates they capture a distinct dimension of complexity - information density and change magnitude - rather than redundantly measuring what cyclomatic complexity already captures. The weak correlation is not a limitation but evidence that entropy provides complementary information. Projects with high cyclomatic complexity from nested control structures may have low entropy if using repetitive patterns, while projects with low cyclomatic complexity may have high entropy from diverse APIs and vocabularies. The validation showing ChatGPT excels in adaptability while entropy-based measures excel in precision and explainability[Adriano Torres et al., 2025] further supports using them in combination: entropy for deterministic measurement and LLMs for interpretation.

**Clone Evolution Consistency Debates.** Earlier evidence suggested 50% consistency in clone change propagation[Tibor Bakota, 2011], while later macro co-change analysis found most cloned files change only with their clone partners[A. Lozano et al., 2014], suggesting higher consistency. This apparent contradiction dissolves when considering temporal granularity: immediate commit transactions may show inconsistency, but macro changes grouping burst patterns over weeks reveal eventual consistency[A. Lozano et al., 2014]. The finding that missed patches average 52 weeks lag[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022] supports this interpretation - clones eventually receive updates, but not in the same transaction. Additionally, variant divergence context matters: short-lived forks for pull requests show higher consistency than long-lived variant forks that intentionally maintain different features.

**Tool Performance by Scale.** Computational performance exhibits clear scaling regimes. For small systems (under 10 variants, under 1M LOC), most approaches are viable. For medium systems (10-100 variants, 1-30M LOC), embedding-based approaches[Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021] and optimized clone detectors like LVMapper[Ming Wu et al., 2019] remain fastest. For large systems (millions of repositories, 30M+ LOC), only highly optimized approaches succeed: the World of Code's parallel Louvain clustering on 51.79M commit groups[Audris Mockus, 2026], LVMapper's scalability to 250M LOC[Ming Wu et al., 2019], or lightweight entropy calculations processing 95 projects[Adriano Torres et al., 2025]. The quadratic runtime growth of drift metrics with variant count[Karl Kegel et al., 2024] illustrates why not all measures scale equally - graph-based approaches face fundamental algorithmic limits that embedding-based approaches avoid.

**Integration and Refactoring Challenges.** Git cherry-pick's 64.4% failure rate versus RePatch's 52.8% success rate on previously failing patches[Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025; Daniel Ogenrwot & John Businge, 2025] demonstrates syntax-based tools' fundamental limitations when variants undergo refactoring. However, RePatch's still-substantial 47.2% failure rate on the subset it attempted indicates even semantic reasoning cannot fully bridge structural divergence. This suggests a hierarchy of patch integration difficulty: (1) trivial context matches handled by Git, (2) refactoring-induced misalignments handled by semantic tools, and (3) fundamental feature divergence requiring manual inspection. Studies reporting high integration success rates often examine category (1) cases, while those reporting lower rates include categories (2) and (3). The 52-week average lag for missed patches[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022] suggests many remain in category (3) indefinitely.

**Validation Context and Generalizability.** Studies validated on open-source projects[Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022; Zhiwen Zheng et al., 2022a; Md Omar Faruk Rokon et al., 2021; Adriano Torres et al., 2025] versus industrial systems[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020; Karl Kegel et al., 2024] show different performance characteristics. Open-source fork networks exhibit specific topology (GitHub's explicit fork relationships) and development patterns (external contributions via pull requests) that may not generalize to industrial clone-and-own development with implicit variant relationships. The set-based comparison's controlled experiment on five forks[Slawomir Duszynski et al., 2020] provides high internal validity but limited external validity compared to observational studies on hundreds of real variants[Poedjadevie Ramkisoen et al., 2022]. Future work should explicitly test whether measures validated in open-source contexts maintain effectiveness in industrial settings with different collaboration patterns and variant lifecycles.
